national/public/government/sovereign debt:
- total amount of money a federal/central government owes
- just like when people borrow money when they dont have enough, governments borrow when they spend more than they earn
- instead of borrowing from a single bank, countries borrow from several sources such as other countries, big financial institutions, and their own citizens
- national debt only includes federal/central governments debt, not local or state debt or citizens debt

why countries borrow money:
- countries cant just print money due to leading to inflation, increase in prices/decrease in purchase power, and economy crash, which lowers trust in the devalued currency and stops or slows foreign investment ie venezuela and zimbabwe
- debt however removes these worries and just adds interest costs to any loaned sum
- budget deficits, so spending more than what is earnt
- a lack of better options:
  - cutting spending: this leads to fewer services and developments and angers/compromises people ie cuts to police forces/military, education, and health for example
  - raising taxes: makes life more difficult for everyone
- invest in growth: roads, schools, hospitals, etc
- handle crises and emergencies such as wildfires or earthquake responses, etc
- pay off existing debt
- control interest rates and inflation:
  - issuing bonds isnt only to borrow money but also to control the economy via controlling inflation, stabilizing currency, and influencing interest rates
    - if inflation is too high then issuing bonds for people to buy can reduce inflation
    - if the economy is too slow then the government can borrow money to inject into the economy and stimulate growth through stimulus, aid, etc

who lends money to countries?:
- domestic lenders:
  - bonds: lend money to the government and get paid back with interest; buying = injecting money to increase inflation, selling = retracting money to decrease inflation
- foreign lenders:
  - bonds
  - emergency loans through IMF(last choice)
- central bank/debt monetization: prints money and uses it to buy bonds; most countries dont do this anymore due to risks of devaluation and hyperinflation

is national debt bad?:
- depends on use and management
- if money continues to be lent to pay back debts then the interest gets too high; debt trap
- too much debt impacts a nations credit rating which makes lending much more difficult and/or leads to higher interest which could lead to a debt crisis and a possibility of defaulting

how much debt is too much?:
- no exact number, but one way to measure is using the debt-to-gdp ratio ie if the ratio is 60% gdp is $100b then its debt is $60b, with 60% or lower being considered a safe level but its not a strict rule
- some countries like the USA have a debt limit which places a ceiling on the % of debt it can accrue and stops lending right there, but the ceiling can be raised to avoid default
